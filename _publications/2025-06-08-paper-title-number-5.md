## Overview

This work presents an **entropy-aware training framework** for distributed Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), addressing the challenges of heterogeneous data distributions and class imbalance across graph partitions. These challenges can adversely affect model convergence and lead to lower predictive performance compared to centralized training.

The proposed framework combines entropy-aware graph partitioning, asynchronous personalization, and class-balanced sampling to improve training efficiency and predictive accuracy. By minimizing the total entropy across graph partitions and adapting models to local data distributions, the approach enables more effective distributed learning on large-scale graphs.

Implemented on the DistDGL framework, the proposed techniques achieve faster convergence, improved predictive performance, and better scalability across large graph datasets.

## Abstract

Several distributed frameworks have been developed to scale Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to billion-scale graphs. However, experiments on several benchmarks reveal that graph partitions generated by these frameworks often exhibit heterogeneous data distributions and class imbalance. These characteristics adversely affect model convergence and can result in lower predictive performance compared to centralized implementations.

To address these challenges, we develop a set of techniques that improve both training efficiency and predictive accuracy. First, we introduce an **Edge-Weighted Partitioning** technique that minimizes the total entropy across graph partitions, thereby improving the micro-averaged F1 score. Second, we incorporate an **asynchronous personalization phase** that adapts each compute host's model to its local data distribution. Finally, we design a **class-balanced sampler** that significantly accelerates model convergence.

We implement the proposed techniques within the DistDGL framework and evaluate their effectiveness across five large-scale graph benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves:

- **2–3× faster training:** Significant reduction in training time compared to standard distributed training baselines.
- **4% average improvement in micro-F1 scores:** Enhanced predictive performance across five large graph benchmarks.
- **Improved scalability:** More efficient distributed training on large-scale graph datasets.

These results demonstrate the effectiveness of entropy-aware partitioning, asynchronous personalization, and class-balanced sampling in improving the efficiency and accuracy of distributed GNN training.

## Key Contributions

- Proposed an **Edge-Weighted Partitioning** technique based on entropy minimization to improve graph partition quality.
- Introduced asynchronous personalization to adapt compute-host models to heterogeneous local data distributions.
- Designed a class-balanced sampling strategy to accelerate model convergence.
- Integrated the proposed techniques into the DistDGL framework for scalable distributed GNN training.
- Achieved **2–3× training speedup** and **4% average micro-F1 improvement** across five large-scale graph benchmarks.
